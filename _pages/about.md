**Job Market Candidate — International Trade, Development, & Firm Behavior**

I am a fourth-year Ph.D. candidate in Economics at the University of Delaware. My research lies in applied microeconomics and examines how policy distortions and institutional constraints shape firm behavior in developing economies.

My job market paper examines how trade liberalization affects manufacturing firms in Pakistan. Using firm-level administrative data and variation in tariff exposure under the EU's GSP+ agreement, I show that liberalization reallocates resources toward more productive firms through within-firm restructuring rather than solely through firm exit.

In a second project, I study Pakistan's 2023 foreign exchange crisis and the government's introduction of barter trade with Afghanistan. Using transaction-level customs data, I find that barter-eligible products saw higher export quantities at the crisis peak—providing the first causal evidence that alternative payment mechanisms can sustain trade when banking channels collapse.

Methodologically, I work extensively with large administrative and survey datasets, applying techniques such as A/B testing, difference-in-differences, instrumental variables, and event study designs to extract causal insights from observational data. I am proficient in Stata, R, and Python, and have experience building data pipelines for cleaning, merging, and analyzing complex datasets.
